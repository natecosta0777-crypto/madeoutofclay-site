If there were a single, simple habit that could grow your child's vocabulary, imagination, empathy, and closeness to you — all at once — wouldn't you want it? Good news: it exists, it's free, and it fits in fifteen minutes. It's reading aloud.

Reading to children is about far more than literacy, though it certainly builds that. When you read aloud, your child hears rich language, explores new worlds, and learns to see life through others' eyes. Stories build empathy in a way lectures never can.

But the deepest gift is connection. Curled up together with a book, your child feels your undivided attention. Your voice becomes a place of safety. Years later, they may not remember the plot, but they'll remember the warmth of being close to you.

Make it easy on yourself. You don't need to read for an hour or use funny voices (though kids love when you do). Fifteen minutes at bedtime is plenty. Keep books within reach — a basket by the bed, a stack on the couch. Let your child pick sometimes, even if it means the same story fifty times.

Choose books that reflect goodness and beauty: tales of courage, kindness, forgiveness, and faith. Mix in picture books, chapter books, and even a children's Bible. Variety keeps it fresh and feeds growing hearts.

Don't stop when they learn to read on their own. Older kids still treasure being read to — it's one of the few times a busy child sits still and simply enjoys being near you.

Tonight, pick a book. Sit close. Read a chapter. You're not just building readers — you're building a bond that lasts a lifetime.
