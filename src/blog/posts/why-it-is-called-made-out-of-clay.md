My son's name is Clay. He is autistic.

I named Made Out of Clay after him so he would know, every day, that his dad is proud of him.

The name came before a catalog, a book club, or a plan for how the stories might grow. It began as a promise: the person at the center of this work would never be treated as a problem to solve.

## The books are for him first

The books, songs, and stories are for Clay first. They make room for quiet days and loud days, big feelings, deep interests, patience, courage, and the relief of being understood without having to perform.

That does not mean every character is a copy of him. It means the world around the characters should carry the same respect I want around my son: differences are part of a whole person, not a lesson pasted onto them for somebody else's benefit.

Children deserve stories where they can recognize themselves without the story turning them into a diagnosis. Families deserve stories that do not ask them to compare one child with another. The goal is not to define one "right" way to think, feel, communicate, play, or grow.

## Representation should feel like welcome

For me, affirming representation is not a perfect speech from a perfect character. It is a child opening a book and finding a world that makes room.

Sometimes that means a character needs more quiet. Sometimes somebody communicates differently, waits longer, feels something intensely, or notices what everybody else missed. The story does not need to cure that difference. It needs to respect the person living it.

That is the standard I keep returning to: **does this story make a child feel smaller, or does it make room for the child to be fully present?**

## What I hope another family finds here

If one other family finds a little peace in these pages, we have done something worthwhile.

Maybe a book gives a parent words for a feeling that was hard to name. Maybe a child sees a character respond to the world in a familiar way. Maybe a read-aloud creates one calm moment at the end of a long day.

No book can promise a clinical, educational, or therapeutic result, and these stories are not a substitute for individual support from qualified professionals. What a story can offer is recognition, language, imagination, and time together.

## This is my love letter to my son

Made Out of Clay is not named for an abstract idea. It is named for Clay.

I want him to grow up knowing that this work exists because his life brought something good into mine—something worth writing down, illustrating, singing, and sharing.

This is my love letter to my son. You are welcome to read along.
