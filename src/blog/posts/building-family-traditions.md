Ask any adult about their favorite childhood memories, and you'll rarely hear about expensive gifts. Instead, you'll hear about traditions — the annual camping trip, the birthday breakfast, the way Mom always sang the same song at Christmas. Traditions are the threads that weave a family together.

The beautiful thing is that traditions don't have to be grand. They just have to be *yours*, and they have to be repeated. A tradition is simply something you do together, again and again, until it becomes part of who your family is.

Start with the calendar you already have. Make birthdays special with a "you are loved" breakfast and a chair decorated with balloons. Turn the first snow into hot cocoa night. Give each holiday a signature activity — baking on Christmas Eve, a gratitude walk at Thanksgiving, sunrise service at Easter.

Then add small everyday traditions, too. Friday pizza night. Sunday afternoon walks. A special handshake at bedtime. These little rituals give children a deep sense of security and belonging — a steady rhythm they can count on.

Traditions also pass down values and faith. When you serve a meal to a neighbor every Christmas, your children learn generosity. When you pray together before trips, they learn to trust God with their days. The things you repeat become the things they remember — and eventually, the things they'll pass to their own children.

You don't need to start ten traditions today. Pick one. Do it with love. Repeat it. In time, it will become part of your family's story — a treasure your children will carry all their lives.
