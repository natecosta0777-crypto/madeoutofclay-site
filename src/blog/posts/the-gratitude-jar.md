Gratitude is one of the greatest gifts we can give our children — and it's also one of the simplest to teach. You don't need a curriculum or a big plan. You just need a jar.

Here's how it works: place an empty jar somewhere central, with slips of paper and a pen nearby. Any time someone notices something to be thankful for — a sunny day, a kind friend, a good meal, a hug — they write it down and drop it in. That's it.

The magic is in the noticing. When children start looking for things to be grateful for, they begin to see the world differently. A rainy day becomes a chance to splash in puddles. A scraped knee becomes a reminder of a caring mom. Gratitude retrains the heart to look for good.

Once a week — maybe at Sunday dinner — pull out a few slips and read them aloud. You'll be amazed at what your kids notice that you missed. Some notes will make you laugh ("thankful for the dog's funny ears"), and some will make your eyes well up ("thankful my brother shared").

This little habit has deep roots. Thankfulness lifts our mood, softens our hearts toward one another, and turns our eyes toward the One who gives every good gift. It's hard to grumble and give thanks at the same time.

Start tonight. Set out a jar. Write the first note yourself: *"Thankful for this family."* Watch how quickly the little hands follow.

*"Give thanks in all circumstances." — 1 Thessalonians 5:18*
