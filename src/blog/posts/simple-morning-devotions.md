Mornings can be chaos — missing shoes, spilled cereal, and a scramble out the door. But even in the rush, a few quiet minutes together can set the whole day on a gentler course.

Family devotions don't have to be long or complicated. In fact, the simpler, the better when little ones are involved. Five minutes is plenty. The goal isn't to fill time; it's to fill hearts.

Try this easy rhythm. Read one short verse or a page from a children's devotional. Talk about it in a sentence or two — *"How can we be kind today?"* Then say a quick prayer together, letting each child add one thing they want to pray for. That's it. Warm, simple, doable.

Keep a basket of tools ready: a kids' Bible, a devotional book, maybe a few worship songs to play while everyone eats. When it's easy to reach, it's easy to keep going.

Don't worry about perfection. Some mornings will feel meaningful; others will be interrupted by a knocked-over juice cup. That's okay. The consistency matters more than the polish. Children remember the rhythm — the sense that in this home, we start the day with God and with each other.

These small moments add up. Over months and years, five minutes a morning becomes a foundation of faith, security, and love. Your children learn that no matter how busy life gets, they are grounded in something steady.

Tomorrow morning, before the rush, gather for just a moment. Read a verse. Say a prayer. Begin the day together.
